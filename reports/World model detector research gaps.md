# Aim the Bellman audit at trust, not reward

**Bottom line.** The best openings for our detector are the places where a world model's mistakes do not fix themselves. That means frozen or pretrained models, safety filters, plan-time exploitation by the optimizer, and choosing how far to trust imagination under a compute budget. Using the detector to tweak the online policy's reward is a poor bet. Our 7 failed attempts fit a pattern the literature already explains: online, a penalty mainly suppresses the exploration that would repair the model. Our strongest asset for a main-conference paper is the horizon finding. Ensemble alarms go quiet while Bellman checks get louder as the rollout gets longer, on 15/15 robots and 30/30 DMC tasks. Two 2026 preprints saw the same thing on a single model family each (Dreamer-style RSSM, and one quadruped model). Nobody has shown it on a decoder-free, value-equivalent model like TD-MPC2, across this many tasks, against a learned detector. The field is crowded and moves month to month. At least three 2026 papers sit very close to us: Hansen & Wang (the TD-MPC2 author, on predicting hallucination), "Operator-on-F" (a Bellman-residual diagnostic on TD-MPC2 checkpoints), and Cao & Bansal (who claim value-consistency residuals stay small when the model is wrong). Operator-on-F was seen only as a snippet. Hansen & Wang and Cao & Bansal were read only in part, and Cao & Bansal's numbers sit in figures the notes could not read. Before we claim novelty, all three have to be read in full by us. My ranking puts first a horizon-indexed benchmark of decision-relevant detectors with a mechanism study, and second a test of whether the planner's own optimization concentrates hallucination. Both are detector-first work, which matches the project's current priority. Planner-gating work comes later, and only under a fixed compute budget, because replanning every step still wins on raw success.

## Why detection has not turned into better policies, here or anywhere

Our negative results are not an outlier. Online model-based RL mostly uses ensemble disagreement to decide when to stop or mask a rollout, not to penalize reward. MACURA truncates rollouts where ensemble uncertainty is high ([MACURA, ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/frauenknecht24a.html)). Infoprop tracks accumulated error and stops the rollout when it is too large, and it reaches **state-of-the-art Dyna-style MuJoCo results with much longer rollouts** ([Infoprop, ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16918v2)). Both live in a different regime from ours. They run SAC on MuJoCo with long imagined rollouts that fill the replay buffer. TD-MPC2 plans only 3 to 5 steps ahead, so there is little imagination to cut. Theory points the same way. Offline pessimism is, by construction, the sign flip of an online exploration bonus ([Jin, Yang, Wang, ICML 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/jin21e.html)). COPlanner quietly concedes the point: it penalizes uncertainty inside the model but rewards it when acting in the real environment ([COPlanner, ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.07220)). TD-MPC2's only built-in uncertainty handling is a test-time penalty on Q-ensemble variance ([TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16828v2)).

Detection papers outside RL show the same gap. A 2026 survey of about 150 robot-policy verifiers finds that the best-known monitors (SAFE, Sentinel, FIPER) report detection and calibration **but not task benefit from acting on the alarm** ([No Free Checker survey, arXiv 2609.09250](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09250)). MEND, a label-free latent hallucination detector on DINO-WM, says plainly that "gains on a downstream controller are left for future work" ([MEND, arXiv 2609.39182](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39182)). So "detector improves policy" is an open problem for everyone, and our 7 careful negatives are a real data point. They belong inside a paper as a section, not as the paper's main claim. No dedicated negative-results paper on online penalties for latent planners turned up, but a negatives-only paper is a hard sell at a main venue.

Our chunking results also have outside support. A 2026 MIT paper from Tedrake's group finds that long open-loop execution mostly helps short-context policies copy non-Markovian demonstrations, and that with enough context **"the most reactive closed-loop policies perform best"** ([Zeng et al., arXiv 2608.15938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15938), abstract only). That matches our +0.10 to +0.15 for replanning every step. The practical lesson is that a commitment gate cannot sell itself on success rate. It has to sell itself on success at a fixed compute or latency budget. Our 1.73x wall-clock speedup is exactly that kind of number, and the 2026 chunking papers report the same kind: FFDC-WAM cuts world-model calls by **69 to 74%** ([arXiv 2605.06222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06222)) and DVAC cuts replanning by **43%** ([arXiv 2606.03847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03847)).

## The horizon finding is our sharpest edge, and two preprints back it

Two independent 2026 preprints found the same pathology we did, each on a narrower setup. **Biased Dreams** (Trimpe and Leibe groups, RWTH Aachen) studies Dreamer-style RSSM models on 4 DMC tasks with a 5-member latent ensemble. It finds that disagreement "does not reliably reflect global compounding model error". For out-of-distribution start states, uncertainty starts high and **quickly falls to in-distribution levels while the true error keeps growing**, which the authors call an attractor effect. They propose no fix and suspect the cause is VAE-style latents ([arXiv 2604.25416](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25416v2)). One note lists it as RLC 2026 and another found no venue, so check before citing. **Right Ranking, Wrong Scale** (one independent author) tests the ensemble on ETH's Robotic World Model. Disagreement ranks error well (Spearman +0.90 at 100 steps) but badly under-states its size. One-sigma coverage is **16% at 1 step and 4.6% at 100 steps, against 68% nominal**. The paper traces this to every member being fed the ensemble mean, and to members sharing 89% of their parameters ([arXiv 2611.02699](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02699)).

Our result goes further in three ways. TD-MPC2 has no decoder, so VAE inductive bias cannot be the whole story. We cover manipulation (ManiSkill3) as well as locomotion. And we test across 45 tasks with a learned Bellman detector alongside. It also explains why MOBILE loses to us (0.728 vs 0.814 AUROC). MOBILE's "Bellman inconsistency" is the spread of one-step Bellman targets across dynamics-ensemble members ([MOBILE, ICML 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/sun23q.html)). If all members collapse onto the same attractor, their Bellman targets agree too. In other words, MOBILE is still an ensemble signal, just measured in value units. It also fits an older finding that ensemble penalties track dynamics error more than distribution shift and "can in fact assign high certainty to data far away from the offline data" ([Lu et al., ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.04135)).

There is also a framing argument. TD-MPC2 is trained to be value-equivalent and has no decoder, so the only way to say it is "hallucinating" is in value or Bellman terms. Pixel predictors cannot even be computed. That makes a Bellman audit the natural detector for this whole model class, and it gives a clean line against Hansen & Wang, whose signals need a tokenizer and a diffusion decoder.

Three threats need answers before submission. First, **"Operator-on-F"** reports that on released TD-MPC2 checkpoints a raw Bellman-operator residual correlates only weakly with return ([alphaXiv 2607.04464](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.04464), snippet only). Our counter is "learned, not raw", but we have to show that with the raw residual as a baseline. Second, Cao & Bansal state that ensemble disagreement and **"value-target consistency residuals" can stay small** while the model is wrong ([arXiv 2609.34300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34300)). Reviewers will quote this against us, so a head-to-head on their ManiSkill vase task is worth the effort. Third, **"The Intervention Gap in Latent World Models"** is reported to show, on TD-MPC2, that return falls as error on task observables grows while reward-prediction error stays flat. It is also said to find that disagreement ranks error only near training support (ρ 0.678 in-support vs 0.027 off-support) ([arXiv 2608.29998](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29998)). One note could not open this PDF at all, and the numbers came from a search summary. This paper could overlap heavily with our horizon claim, so it is the most urgent unknown.

## Where the 2026 field has left gaps a learned audit fills

### The gates and monitors trust the imagination they read

Adaptive chunking became a crowded subfield in 2026, and its world-model branch is the closest to our imagined-regret (IR) gate. CA3C imagines several candidate chunks and **commits while the imagined futures agree** ([arXiv 2610.07949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07949v1), abstract only). FFDC-WAM checks whether the imagined future still matches reality ([arXiv 2605.06222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06222)). DREAM-Chunk runs the chunk whose dreamed state best matches the observed state ([arXiv 2606.18589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18589)). On the critic side, ACSAC picks the chunk size with the highest predicted value ([arXiv 2605.11009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11009)) and DEHP learns a horizon head with RL ([arXiv 2606.11408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11408)). In the abstracts, none of them checks whether the world model or critic is itself reliable at the horizon it commits to.

That blind spot matters, because world models turn out to be optimistic. A 2026 study of released robot world models finds they predict success for failed actions **80% of the time after official fine-tuning** ([World Models Dream of Success, arXiv 2610.09134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09134)). An agreement gate like CA3C will then over-commit exactly when every imagined future shares the same mistake. Our horizon result predicts the same: agreement gets more confident as the rollout gets longer. FFDC-WAM and DREAM-Chunk are close relatives of our prediction-error (PE) baseline, so "IR versus PE" alone is not new enough. Our honest result (IR ties PE except at long commitment on grounded models with extra heads) is a supporting finding, not a headline.

Failure monitors share the blind spot. Foresight scores the policy's proposed chunk using latents from a frozen V-JEPA 2 world model, and gains **+0.14 balanced accuracy on BEHAVIOR-1K** ([arXiv 2606.23085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23085v1)). ContactGuard, FARM and PATCH do similar things ([2608.13438](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13438), [2609.11445](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11445), [2606.16690](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16690); all abstract only). So "predict failure ahead of time from world-model imagination" is no longer a gap. The gap one level up is still open: these monitors read world-model latents as if they were true. The No Free Checker survey calls this the "model-intrinsic blind spot" ([arXiv 2609.09250](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09250)).

### Safety filters give guarantees only relative to a model they cannot audit

World-model safety filters have settled on one recipe: an uncertainty or residual score, a conformal threshold, then a pessimistic safety filter. The score is always one step long. UNISafe uses a separate ensemble's one-step disagreement and cuts block-plucking failure from **0.41 with no filter to 0.20**. Its stated limitation is that "the separate ensemble may not faithfully capture the world model's true uncertainty" ([UNISafe, CoRL 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00779v1)). ACoFi calibrates the observed errors of a learned safety value online ([arXiv 2604.18482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18482)). Cao & Bansal use the one-step residual between the encoded latent and the predicted latent, so their method is **reactive** by its own account: it needs the real observation first ([arXiv 2609.34300](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34300)). None of them gives a pre-execution, horizon-indexed score for whether the multi-step safety rollout the filter relies on is wrong. Our horizon result also offers a mechanism for UNISafe's limitation and for Cao & Bansal's observation that the UNISafe score stayed low until collision. The closest TD-MPC2 safety work, MUARL, adds evidential uncertainty to TD-MPC2's planner ([ICLR 2026 listing](https://www.iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10018079), abstract only). Nobody has checked whether that uncertainty grows with horizon.

### The optimizer exploits model error, and nothing detects it at plan time

Model exploitation now has a formal definition with no detector attached. "Imperfect World Models are Exploitable" (NeurIPS 2026) shows exploitation is essentially unavoidable on large policy sets but admits a "safe horizon" ([arXiv 2605.15960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15960)). A dithering paper shows a TD-MPC2 planner drifting toward actions that a persistent quantization error favours ([arXiv 2611.11780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11780), snippet only). Best-of-N with a world-model verifier **can saturate or get worse as N grows** ([arXiv 2607.17454](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17454), snippet only), the classic sign of a verifier being gamed. The defenses so far are horizon limits, repairing the model from failure rollouts, and DreamLedger. DreamLedger needs a history of execution outcomes keyed by horizon and cuts burn rate by **4.8 points versus random refusal** ([arXiv 2608.23863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23863)). No paper measures whether hallucination is concentrated in the plans the optimizer actually selects compared with random candidates, and none offers a history-free plan-time signal for it. The workshop paper "Decision-Level Auditing" found that a prediction-only near-tie score detects selector switches (AUROC .714) but **does not identify higher regret** ([NeurIPS 2026 workshop](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/165855)). That is the piece a learned audit plus IR targets.

### Frozen foundation world models ship with no reliability check

The pretrained latent planners (DINO-WM, V-JEPA 2-AC, JEPA-WMs) plan with CEM or gradient descent on a plain embedding-distance cost, and **none uses uncertainty inside the planner**. The JEPA-WMs authors note that "good rollout accuracy does not guarantee planning success", and they release code, data and checkpoints ([arXiv 2512.24497](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24497v2)). DINO-WM's decoded final latent can show success when the simulator shows a miss ([arXiv 2512.09929](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.09929), snippet only). Hansen's 200+ released Newt checkpoints carry a model-card warning that they are not expected to generalise to unseen tasks without fine-tuning ([Newt model card](https://huggingface.co/nicklashansen/newt)). Frozen models are exactly where errors do not self-correct, which makes them the setting where our online failures matter least.

### 1. Ensembles go quiet, Bellman audits get louder: a benchmark and a mechanism

**Gap.** No peer-reviewed work measures detector quality as a function of horizon across many tasks for decoder-free latent models. None compares a learned Bellman audit against the 2026 label-free signals, raw Bellman residuals, and ensembles on the same labels. **Who is nearby:** Trimpe/Frauenknecht (RWTH), Hansen & Wang (UCSD), the Operator-on-F and Intervention Gap authors, and Yang Yu's group (MOBILE). **Why it is ours:** we already have the 15-robot and 30-task horizon curves, plus a detector that beats MOBILE. **What lifts it to a main-conference paper** is the mechanism, not more tasks. Test the Right Ranking, Wrong Scale explanation (members fed the ensemble mean, with shared trunks) on TD-MPC2. Show whether rolling members independently, or using separately trained ensembles, slows the collapse. A short section on the 7 failed policy-improvement attempts goes in as "why the detector belongs in trust decisions, not rewards". **Risks:** Operator-on-F or Intervention Gap may already own part of this. Reviewers may say the Bellman check rises only because the true value error rises. Answer that with per-horizon AUROC against fixed labels, and by matching base rates per horizon. **First experiment:** on seed-40 ManiSkill3 plus 5 DMC tasks, compute AUROC at horizons 1 through H for six signals: the learned audit, the raw Bellman residual (an Operator-on-F stand-in), MOBILE, TD-MPC2's own Q-ensemble variance, a 5-member separately trained dynamics ensemble, and the one-step latent residual (a Cao & Bansal stand-in). Then rerun the ensemble with independently rolled members to test the mechanism. All of this runs on saved rollouts with no new agent training.

### 2. Does the planner pick the hallucinations? Optimization-induced exploitation

**Gap.** Exploitation theory has no detector ([2605.15960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15960)), best-of-N is known to degrade, and DreamLedger needs execution history. Nobody has measured whether the plans the optimizer selects are more hallucinated than random candidates. **Who is nearby:** Kochenderfer/Ramamoorthy/Abel (theory), the DreamLedger authors, and the dithering paper on TD-MPC2. **Our prediction:** as optimization pressure rises, the hallucination rate of the selected plan rises, the audit score rises with it, and ensemble disagreement does not, because the optimizer steers into attractor regions where the ensemble agrees. That is a crisp, falsifiable claim tying our horizon law to planning, and it does not depend on the detector improving the policy. **Risks:** TD-MPC2's MPPI warm-start and policy prior may weaken the effect, and a null result is possible. Labels for the selected plan require running it open-loop in the simulator, which is cheap. **First experiment:** on seed-40, sweep MPPI iterations (1, 2, 4, 6, 12), the number of samples, and best-of-N over sampled plans. For each setting, run the selected plan and 10 random candidates open-loop in a cloned simulator state. Plot the real hallucination rate, the audit score and the ensemble score against optimization pressure. If the gap opens, add a "trust-weighted argmax" that drops plans the audit flags, and compare success at matched compute.

### 3. Warn before the safety filter trusts a wrong imagination

**Gap.** UNISafe, ACoFi and Cao & Bansal all use one-step or reactive signals. Cao & Bansal explicitly dismiss value-consistency residuals. A pre-execution, horizon-indexed, conformally calibrated audit is missing ([UNISafe](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00779v1), [Cao & Bansal](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34300)). **Who is nearby:** Bajcsy (CMU), Bansal (USC), Sibai (ACoFi), Lindemann and Deshmukh (conformal reachability). **Our difference:** we warn before acting, at a chosen horizon, with a score that does not collapse. The payoff metrics are fewer violations, fewer needless interventions, and more lead time. That suits a detector that does not raise task success. A conformal help-budget variant fits here too ("ask a human when even the replanned rollout is flagged"). No world-model detector drives conformal help requests for MPC agents yet; KnowNo does this for language-model planners ([arXiv 2307.01928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928)). **Risks:** this needs a cost-constrained TD-MPC2 setup we do not have. Conformal guarantees break when rollouts are not exchangeable under shift. Bansal's and Bajcsy's labs are fast and could get there first. **First experiment:** build a ManiSkill3 pick-and-place with a forbidden region, like their vase task. Add friction, mass and timestep shifts. Use a simple rule: if the audit at horizon h exceeds a conformal threshold, fall back to a safe policy. Compare against UNISafe-style ensemble divergence and a one-step residual with online conformal calibration. Report violations, false interventions, lead time, and empirical coverage against nominal.

### 4. Audit frozen and pretrained latent world models, including unseen tasks

**Gap.** Frozen models cannot self-correct, Newt ships with a generalisation warning, and JEPA-WM and DINO-WM planners have no reliability check. The decision-level audit workshop paper found a prediction-only score that misses regret ([NeurIPS 2026 workshop](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/165855)). **Who is nearby:** Meta FAIR/LeCun (JEPA-WMs, stable-worldmodel), Hansen (Newt), and the MEND authors. **Our difference:** a learned head trained on frozen latents to predict decision-relevant error at horizon h, tested on whether a head trained on seen tasks flags unseen ones. That turns project 1 into a tool people can use on any released checkpoint. **Risks:** a cross-family port (DINO-WM, JEPA-WM) costs engineering time and uses a goal-distance cost instead of Q values, so "Bellman" becomes "realized vs predicted goal distance". DINO-WM checkpoint availability is unconfirmed. **First experiment:** stay inside the TD-MPC2 family first. Take released Newt or TD-MPC2 multitask checkpoints, roll out on held-out DMC tasks, label by real-vs-imagined Bellman error, train the audit on half the tasks, and report AUROC by horizon on the other half against ensemble and MOBILE. Port to JEPA-WMs Push-T or Wall only if transfer holds.

### 5. Audited commitment under a compute budget

**Gap.** The world-model chunking gates trust their imagination, and none compares regret, agreement and prediction-error gating on the same benchmark. **Who is nearby:** the CA3C authors, Xiaojuan Qi (FFDC-WAM), Yuejiang Liu (DREAM-Chunk), Garg/Shkurti (DEHP), and Levine/Physical Intelligence (RTC, Decoupled Q-Chunking). **Our angle:** the "shared hallucination" test. Find rollouts where ensemble latent variance is low but the audit flags error, commit anyway, and measure the real outcome, using our grounded/stock 2x2 to vary how much the model hallucinates. Frame success at a fixed compute budget, never raw success, because replanning every step beats every gate. **Risks:** IR does not transfer to stock TD-MPC2 and only wins at long commitment on grounded models with extra heads, so the positive result is narrow. The benchmarks reviewers expect (LIBERO, RoboTwin with pi0.5) differ from ours. The project memory also says detector work comes before planner work, so this should follow project 1. **First experiment:** on the 2x2 models, compare fixed 4 and 6 step commitment, PE gating, IR gating, a CA3C-style agreement gate, and IR combined with the audit, under object-pose and friction shifts. Plot success against wall-clock per episode as a curve, with replan-every-step as the ceiling.

### 6. Trust scores for imagination-based policy evaluation

**Gap.** World-model evaluators are judged on aggregate rank correlation. WorldGym, for example, lands within 3.3% of real success rates ([WorldGym, ICLR 2026](https://world-model-eval.github.io/abstract)). Individual verdicts are unreliable: world models are optimistic about failures ([arXiv 2610.09134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09134)), and video world models "self-correct" failures into successes ([dWorldEval, ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/65898)). **Our angle:** throw out audit-flagged imagined rollouts and test whether checkpoint ranking against true simulator return improves. It is cheap, because the simulator gives ground truth for free. But it sits far from the video-world-model community that cares most about evaluation, so it works better as a section of project 1. **First experiment:** take 20 checkpoints from one ManiSkill3 training run. Estimate each one's return in imagination with and without audit filtering, and report Spearman correlation with true return and the false-success rate.

### 7. Audit-guided data collection

**Gap.** Biased Dreams suggests disagreement-driven exploration such as Plan2Explore ([arXiv 2005.05960](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.05960)) under-samples the regions where long-horizon imagination fails. Nobody has tested this. **Overlap:** Hansen & Wang already own "hallucination predictor as curiosity", in pixel space. The project memory notes the professor's interest in reward-free, per-signal GVF critics (Horde), and a horizon-indexed audit used as a data-acquisition score fits that direction. **Risks:** this is closest to our 7 failures, and a Bellman signal may also fire on noisy rewards (the noisy-TV problem). **First experiment:** in 3 DMC tasks with a fixed environment-step budget, collect data four ways: by ensemble disagreement, by audit score, by RND, and randomly. Score each by imagined-vs-true error at horizon H on held-out states, not by return, and add a noisy-distractor control.

## Conclusion

The literature changes how our results read. The 7 failures and the replanning result are not signs of a weak detector. They show that online, the agent's own data collection already fixes most model errors, so a detector earns its keep only where that repair loop is missing: frozen models, safety-critical actions, optimizer pressure, and tight compute. That turns the paper from "a detector that helps the policy" into "a horizon law about which error signals stay honest, and the decisions that law should govern." The claim of ours that nobody else can make is the 45-task, decoder-free horizon law with a learned detector that keeps its signal. Project 2's prediction (that the optimizer selects the hallucinations the ensemble cannot see) is the cheapest way to turn that law into a mechanism-level result.

The order of work follows from that. Read Operator-on-F, Intervention Gap and Hansen & Wang first, because any one of them could narrow the claim. Then run project 1's six-signal horizon comparison and project 2's optimization sweep on saved seed-40 rollouts. Both reuse what already exists and need no new agent training. Safety (project 3) is the strongest follow-on for CoRL or RSS once the core law holds. Planner gating should wait, and should only ever be judged on success per unit of compute.
