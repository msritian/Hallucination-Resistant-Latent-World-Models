**Reading.**
- Errors that change the decision are the costly ones. Gating on them (act) beats gating on the return-error label
  (oracle) by +0.048, and the critic-regret oracle (reg) does better still: 0.722 at 4 actions per plan, against 0.743
  for replanning at every step (k1), at a quarter of the planning compute.
- IR, the training-free imagined-regret signal, captures most of the act oracle's gain before acting:
  IR − act = −0.012 [−0.040, +0.018]. It beats random by +0.091 and the LBA by +0.055.
- The return-error oracle now beats random (+0.054, CI above 0), unlike the main study (+0.029, CI touching 0). The
  ranking oracle < act < reg holds.
- Accuracy and control disagree. On return-error labels the LBA is the most accurate before-acting score (AUROC 0.891),
  but on decision labels every score is weak (0.64-0.73). IR is only 0.758 / 0.680 yet is the best before-acting gate
  for control.
